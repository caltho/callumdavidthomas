Round 2 Latest

A person.

Route A's landing area, then Route B's calm lists redrawn in A's style. Each section below the fold is a ruled strip: a margin column with a label that stays put as you scroll, then open lists with only horizontal rules. The boxes stop at the fold.

The idea running through it: in a world where the machines keep getting smarter, you're a real person. Not artificial. Occasionally intelligent. Can feel things, man. It also has things to fiddle with, just because.

  • Intro line"A person." with a dictionary entry: 1. a real, living human, not artificial, occasionally intelligent; 2. Callum Thomas, builder, AI whisperer, solution seeker. Can feel things, man.
  • FidgetsA name that tires the more you stir it (some dots never go home), an "I'm not a robot" checkbox, a slide-to-prove-humanity slider, a stampable stamp, and a scratch pad that draws in the colour of whatever you last clicked.
  • New postOfficially Australian. Needs the two ceremony photos in public/images/citizenship/.
  • WordmarkGlides in from a distance (small, soft and faint, then sharp). No bounce. Then the text settles in.
  • Ask CallumAn AI-assistant parody with a model picker. Callum 1.0 is "human, thoughtful, replies within a day". callum-nano "Thought for 0.4 seconds". The small print says "Callum can make mistakes. He's only human."
  • Proof of humanityOff the clock, reframed as evidence: sleep, altitude, jiu-jitsu, the boat, art, and the local time. Ends with an "I'm not a robot either" checkbox.
  • Small jokes"Not AI-generated" photo captions, "Checked: Claude (not human)" in the title block, the human check in the facts column.

The routes

Route A

Sheet

The most literal take. The whole page is one engineering drawing: hairline cells like Heron, crop marks and a ruler around your name, and a title block at the bottom stamped Issued for hire.

Your CV is a revision table (Rev A, graduating, up to Rev D, RJE). It's the most "designed", and it's the one where the engineering background is most obviously the concept.

  • WordmarkHero, round dots, orange when disturbed
  • AccentSignal orange, close to your old oxide red
  • TypeInter Tight + JetBrains Mono
  • Intro"Builder. AI whisperer. Solution seeker."
  • RiskDense. Lots of boxes can start to feel like a form.

Route B

Register

The calmest and quickest to read. Lots of white space, horizontal rules only, and one big sentence in your voice. Closest to United Carriers.

All 17 projects sit in a drawing register you can filter (Live, Work & clients, Personal), with a screenshot that follows the cursor. The wordmark signs off the footer, and the Yukhin climb and your sleep are both drawn in the same dot language.

Route C

Markup

The warmest. It opens like a letter ("Hi, I'm Callum.") with hi-vis highlighter on the key phrases, and your photo is annotated like a drawing detail: numbered callouts, a key, and a dimension line reading 1 Callum, not to scale.

The CV runs down a road alignment with chainages. Your stack is a schedule of materials. It ends on a dark band with the wordmark in square "pixel" dots.

  • WordmarkFooter, square dots on ink, hi-vis when disturbed
  • AccentHi-vis highlighter yellow
  • TypeBricolage Grotesque + DM Mono
  • Intro"Hi, I'm Callum." plus a short letter
  • RiskThe jokes are doing more work, and Bricolage has more character than the pure Swiss grotesk you picked.
